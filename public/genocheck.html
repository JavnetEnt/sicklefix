GenoCheck

Choose each partner's genotype to see the possible outcomes for their children. Nothing you choose is saved or sent anywhere.

Each box is one equally likely outcome.

    What do the genotypes mean?

    AA

    No sickle or C gene. Normal haemoglobin.

    AS and AC (carriers)

    One normal gene and one sickle (S) or C gene. Carriers usually stay well, but can pass the gene on. AS is also called sickle cell trait.

    SS

    Two sickle genes. This is sickle cell anaemia, the most common form of sickle cell disease in Nigeria.

    SC

    One sickle gene and one C gene. This is also a form of sickle cell disease and can cause pain crises and complications.

    CC

    Two C genes. This is haemoglobin C disease, which is different from sickle cell disease and is usually milder. A doctor can explain more.

    Not sure of your genotype?

    A simple blood test (haemoglobin electrophoresis) at a hospital or laboratory gives the answer. Rarer types exist too, such as beta thalassaemia, so ask a doctor to read your result. Find a clinic.

    GenoCheck is for awareness and is not medical advice. Chances are the same for every pregnancy: earlier children do not change them.
