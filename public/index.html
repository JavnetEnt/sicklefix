SICKLE CELL IN NIGERIA

Nigeria has more people living with sickle cell disease than any other country. Behind the numbers are people fighting an invisible battle. Let's understand it, together.

2. What is happening inside the body?

Healthy red blood cells are round and soft. They glide through the smallest blood vessels carrying oxygen. In sickle cell disease, the haemoglobin is faulty, so cells can turn stiff and curved like a sickle. They stick together and block blood flow. Tissue is starved of oxygen, and that causes sudden, severe pain and can damage organs over time.

3. Common triggers

  • Dehydration and not drinking enough water
  • Cold, harmattan dryness, or sudden temperature changes
  • Infections, including malaria
  • Extreme stress, exhaustion or overexertion
  • Low oxygen, such as high altitude

Sometimes a crisis comes with no clear cause at all.

4. When it is an emergency

Go to a hospital immediately if a warrior has:
  • Fever (38°C or higher)
  • Chest pain or difficulty breathing
  • Pain not relieved by usual medicine
  • Sudden weakness, trouble speaking, or severe headache
  • Swollen, very painful belly, or sudden severe paleness or yellowing of the eyes
  • A painful erection lasting more than 2 to 4 hours
Don't wait. Delay can be dangerous. Take the warrior's medical records or emergency card if you have one.

5. How to support a warrior

"I believe you. What do you need right now?"
"Let me get you water, a warm blanket, or take you to the hospital."
"Take the time you need. I've got this."
"But you look fine." Pain here is often invisible.
"It's just spiritual/it's your own fault." SCD is a genetic condition, not a curse.
"Stop pretending to get attention." Crisis pain is real, and often undertreated.

6. Know your genotype

Sickle cell is inherited. A child gets one haemoglobin gene from each parent. See what couples could pass on.

Other genotypes (AC, SC) exist too. A genotype test at a hospital or lab, ideally before marriage and in pregnancy, gives you the real answer. Ask a doctor or genetic counsellor about your result.

7. For every warrior

  • Stay hydrated, dress warm, and rest enough
  • Take prescribed medicines and keep clinic appointments
  • Talk about how you feel; your mental health matters
  • Find your people; you are not alone
